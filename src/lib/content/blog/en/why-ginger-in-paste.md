# Why Ginger Belongs in Golden Paste

Doug English's original paste had no ginger. It was added later — on a subscriber's suggestion, as Veterinary Secrets tells it — and the idea stuck: 1 tablespoon of ground ginger per homemade batch.

## What gingerol brings

- **Gut comfort:** ginger is traditionally used against nausea — car sickness, vomiting, bloating. For travel-sick dogs this is the paste's most practical bonus.
- **Anti-inflammatory support:** gingerol is studied as a gentle anti-inflammatory companion — it doesn't replace curcumin, it stands next to it.
- **Taste:** warm spice makes the paste more interesting for picky noses.

## Who it's best for

Seniors with sensitive digestion and dogs that hate car rides. That's why we kept ginger in our recipe instead of returning to the "pure" classic.

## When to be careful

- Ginger is warming and stimulating: always start at half servings for 7–10 days.
- With reflux tendencies, ulcer history, pregnancy, before surgery — vet first.
- Soft stool or vomiting after starting = dose too high or stomach not ready. Lower, watch, ask the vet.

In our jar, ginger is weighed at the same ratio as the vet recipes — no "generous hand".
