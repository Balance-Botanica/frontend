# Golden Paste for Dogs: the Classic Recipe + Our 95% Upgrade

Golden paste is the most famous homemade turmeric recipe for dogs. Vet Doug English popularized it, and creators **Veterinary Secrets** (modified paste with ginger) and **Lolahemp** (step-by-step guide with dosing) broke it down minute by minute. We took both videos as the base — and added what a home kitchen can't.

## The classic base (from both videos)

- Ground turmeric: 1/4–1/2 cup
- Water: 1/2–1 cup, simmer 7–10 min until thick
- Coconut oil: 1/4 cup, stirred in warm (never boiled!)
- Ground black pepper: 1 tsp — the absorption key
- Veterinary Secrets modification: 1 tbsp of ground ginger

Homemade storage: fridge, 2–4 weeks (2 is the safer bet).

## What we changed and why

1. **95% curcumin extract on top of the powder.** Raw turmeric is only 2–5% curcumin, and an "eyeballed" spoon differs every time. Every batch of ours hits **~60 mg curcuminoids per teaspoon**, printed on the jar.
2. **Measured pepper.** A "pinch" is not a dose. Our piperine is weighed: enough for absorption, minimal for sensitive stomachs.
3. **Kept the ginger** — gingerol supports gut comfort and nausea, confirmed by creators and kitchen practice alike. But ginger is warming, so always start at half servings.
4. **Sunflower lecithin** as emulsifier — honestly: not a "phytosome", just keeps fat and water together.
5. **Heavy-metal COA** for every batch: lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury. Turmeric is a classic adulteration target.

## Honest expectations

Paste is an adjunct for joint comfort and mobility — not "natural Rimadyl". Dog studies (Colitti 2012, PMID 22591841; Innes 2003, PMID 12723628; Comblain 2017, PMID 29262825; Caterino 2021, PMID 34048452) show a modest effect: easier rising, better vet/owner scores — but no replacement for prescribed NSAIDs. Medication decisions stay with your vet.
